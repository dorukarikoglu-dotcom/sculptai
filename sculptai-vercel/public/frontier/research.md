# Frontier thesis kill-test — 2026-08-29

Goal: find AI-native transaction businesses with materially larger ceilings than single digital products. Core filter: the agent must own or materially influence an economic loop and monetize when money moves.

## Survived

### Cashflow Foundry — 92/100
AI creates, tests and operates digital businesses until cash flow is verified, then sells the business as an asset. Market evidence: Acquire reports 500k+ entrepreneurs, $500M+ closed deal volume and 2,000+ startups sold. Flippa's H1 2026 report says ~597k registered buyers represent an estimated $120B in acquisition capital; searches for AI-powered businesses rose 20% half-on-half, and AI Apps & Tools recorded its first 14 sales at an average price of $535,714. The wedge is not an AI business-builder subscription: the foundry owns execution and sells proven founder-independent assets.

Kill criteria: cannot produce repeatable revenue without founder/manual hidden labor; buyer intent under 3% after 100 qualified M&A-buyer visits; asset cannot meet marketplace diligence expectations; acquisition value fails to exceed cumulative build/test cost by at least 3x.

### StayBid — 89/100
Demand-side hotel marketplace. Traveler states intent once; AI negotiates directly with hotels and ranks total value. Evidence: a 2026 hotel-negotiation project reports 22 real negotiations and found value often appears in breakfast, credits, cancellation and upgrades rather than headline rate. Rateflow states OTA commissions are commonly 15–25% and pitches a 3–5% direct-booking fee to hotels. A Business Insider experiment also demonstrated an AI agent can call hotels and negotiate a real rate, albeit with small savings.

Kill criteria: fewer than 10% of contacted independent hotels return a materially differentiated offer; total incremental value is under 5% of booking value; traveler intent-to-use under 8% after 100 qualified high-AOV travel visitors; hotel-funded economics cannot beat OTA economics while supporting acquisition costs.

### EarnMore — 83/100
Outcome-priced career agent. Search, filtering, tailored applications, follow-up, interview preparation and user-approved negotiation; monetization only on verified compensation uplift where legally permitted. Existing AI job agents validate automation demand, but most charge subscriptions. CoBlack cites survey results where 67% would delegate job search, 65% resume updating and 55% applications, while only 47–49% would delegate salary/benefit negotiation — therefore trust and approval boundaries are the key product risk.

Kill criteria: jurisdiction makes candidate success-fee model impractical; fewer than 5% of qualified jobseekers accept the proposed success-fee economics; autonomous application quality produces low interview yield or misrepresentation risk; candidate distrust blocks negotiation delegation.

## Killed before launch

### Generic all-in-one refund / consumer-rights agent
Killed as an originality wedge. ReceiptCash already markets an all-in-one AI consumer-rights agent covering refunds, price drops, warranties, recalls, flight compensation, missing parcels, fees and more.

### Generic AI bill negotiator
Killed as a greenfield idea. Dip already uses a voice agent to call providers and negotiate internet, wireless and other recurring bills; LunaBill is described as scanning bills and splitting savings with users, and other players exist.

### Generic creator talent agent
Killed as a greenfield idea. Ephemera offers an AI talent agent at a 3% cut; Vuso is commission-only at 10–15%; CreaSeed says it finds and negotiates brand deals. The market is valid but the take-rate wedge is already occupied.

## Next test order
1. Cashflow Foundry: buyer-side commitment plus production of one genuinely revenue-bearing asset.
2. StayBid: manually/agentically negotiate one real high-AOV stay and quantify total value vs OTA/direct published offer.
3. EarnMore: candidate commitment test; legal model review before any outcome fee is charged.
